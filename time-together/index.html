Time, Together · № 001

The first gathering is a conference.

One hundred people who make and study time — watchmakers, circadian scientists, industrial designers, researchers of every kind of hour — on a west-facing shore, watching the sun set into the water together.

— sunsets until we gather
No clock times

The sunset is the keynote.

Nothing here is scheduled by a numbered grid. Every session runs on solar time — Sunset −3h, Blue Hour, After Dark — because the day is shaped by the sky, not the other way around.

Everything before the sunset builds toward it. Everything after it glows from it. It happens whether we see it or not — on a grey day, the horizon still dims, and the toast still happens at civil twilight.

The program

One day, told by the sun.

  • Sunset −3hDoors. Sundial check-in, coffee, and a horology exhibit — watchmakers bring pieces, table by table.
  • Sunset −2h30Opening: why we gathered, and the Paragonday worldview.
  • Sunset −2hTalks: a circadian scientist and a time researcher. No Q&A — save it for the walks.
  • Sunset −1h15Golden Hour panel: watchmakers × industrial designers on the instruments of time.
  • Sunset −25mWalk to the water. Drinks in hand, blankets handed out.
  • SunsetThe Sunset. Silence. No phones. The disc takes about three minutes to cross the horizon.
  • Blue HourA toast at civil twilight, and the Paragonday moment — the sky still glowing.
  • After DarkDinner by candle and firelight. Amber, circadian-respecting light only.
  • NightFire circle, stargazing, unstructured. The people who stay are the ones you want to know.
The details

A conference that begins a cohort.

This is the first event of Time, Together — and from the hundred people at the water's edge, eight will be invited to continue: four seasonal long weekends, one continuing conversation about what time feels like when you look up for it.
