## The email

Aaron —

Really glad you were on the call today. What you sketched — a whole week of the
challenge given over to time, and a Paragonday-coded invitation to go with it —
is the most concrete adoption of Horizon Time anyone has proposed, and it's
happening in three weeks. So let's make it official.

**The money.** Daylight Computer gave Paragonday a $10,000 donation, currently
held by Other Internet as our fiscal sponsor. I'd like to send $500 of it to you
as a starter stipend for bringing Horizon Time into the DC cohort's week on
time, September 17–24. You raised the number and I think it's right: it's small
enough to stay a collaboration rather than a contract, and it does the thing you
named — it leaves a paper trail.

**What it's for.** Not a deliverables list I'm holding you to. It's the reason
the money moved, written down:

1. **Time Card** — the paper carrier that replaces the big-tech calendar for the
   week. Sun times, the week's plan, the numbers you can't look up anymore, and
   the habit tracker, on one folded page. We have a generator; the current draft
   is Brooklyn and we're regenerating it for DC before the 17th.
2. **Text service** — text `SUNSET` to a number, get the sunset time back. You
   said one keyword is enough and I agree. We're looking at whether it can ride
   on the toll-free number you already use for voicemail.
3. **Sunset field trip** — the pair-up outing, with an invitation that carries
   no clock time on it. It says *meet at tilset −1:00*, so showing up requires
   finding out when the sun sets. You pick the site.
4. **Horizon time habit** — one commitment tethered to the sky instead of a
   number. Wi-Fi off at pastset. Clocks covered at pastset +2:00. Whatever the
   person picks.

Pick the ones that actually fit your cohort and drop the rest. If only the time
card and the walk happen, that's a successful pilot.

**What I'd love back, whenever it's natural.** Not a report — evidence that the
thing was real:

- A handful of photos, or scans of whatever people drew
- Two or three participant lines about how their sense of time changed
- Your own paragraph on what worked and what didn't, especially the didn't
- Any printed artifact we can photograph

The reason I'm asking is straightforward: I want to tell Daylight that their
money put a paper time card in the hands of real people in DC, and I'd rather
show them than describe it.

**Logistics.** The funds sit with Other Internet, so there's a step on their
side to release them — I'm looping them in on this thread so we can sort out
whether they need an invoice or a W-9 from you, and where to send it. Would like
to have it out before the cohort starts September 3rd.

Two last things. First, the standing offer from the call: if Buzz gets
frustrating at any point, say so out loud in the channel and someone will get
you unstuck. The whole point is that you don't have to hold the *how*. Second,
DC is the origin cohort and 1-844-FLIPOFF already exists, which makes you the
right pilot for the text service rather than us — so tell us what you want it to
do and we'll build to that.

Thank you for taking this on.

— Yatú
